Pass L · 14 August 2026 · all four threads shipped · two decisions open

The chrome was quiet for five passes.

Five UI passes executed one doctrine, and executed it well. That is exactly why the app reads as tasteful rather than stunning. This is where the doctrine gets deliberately broken — and where it doesn’t.

The diagnosis

theme.dart states the doctrine outright: “a wallpaper browser is a wall of other people’s colour, so the chrome around it stays deliberately quiet.” Passes A, B, C, D and H all served it — justified rows replacing masonry, an opaque gradient bar after frosted glass failed on the tablet, a shimmer skeleton, tile density, a blurred letterbox fill, unified metadata tiles, long-press quick actions.

Every one of those was the right call. But restraint done well is taste, and nothing more. Stunning comes from authored moments — colour, depth, motion — and the doctrine forbids all three by construction.

So pass L is not a sixth polish pass. It spends colour only where a single photo is already the subject, and motion everywhere. One rule holds throughout, and it is not negotiable:

The browse wall stays neutral

Every hard-won argument in the handoff is about not competing with the wall — the frosted bar rejected on device, C2’s scrim held at .45, C4’s pill alpha left alone. A slice that tints the browse grid has misread this pass.

Confirmed

The purity warning survives the tint. This was the one worth checking on hardware rather than trusting to a test. A sketchy wallpaper’s download icon measured rgb(252,180,189) against its own tinted pane; both safe wallpapers measured no red at all. The defect is fixed in the running app, not only in the suite.

Confirmed

The neutral rule works on live data. The warm wallpaper’s palette came back black, white, brown, tan, grey — the two neutrals skipped and the tint taken from the brown. That is the unit test’s claim, on a real response.

Not a defect

#424153 tints to almost exactly tela’s own accent. The palette’s one muted slate seeds #c3c0ff, beside the app default #b8c6ee — close enough that a casual eye reads the screen as untinted, though the surfaces did move. Recorded so nobody reports it as the tint failing, and so nobody “fixes” it by rejecting that colour: a muted wallpaper earning muted chrome is the honest answer, and rejecting it drops the only tint a greyish photo can have.

Still unjudged

None of it visible in a screenshot: the 450 ms transition itself, the phone layout, and C2’s melt case — no wallpaper in this pass had edge pixels close enough to its own blurred backdrop to test it.

What the build found

Fixed

The tint was repainting the purity warning. The download affordance is painted in ColorScheme.error to say this wallpaper is not safe — and that role’s own docstring already claimed it was the one warm colour that could never be repainted into the thing it warns against. True of the accent splice by construction; false the moment a scheme is grown wholly from the wallpaper’s seed. A dark-red wallpaper reshaded its own warning.

The four error roles are now held back. It was found by an existing test going red, not by inspection.

Fixed

The tint needs a Builder, and only one test proves it. The theme is returned by the screen’s build, so anything read off the screen’s own context resolves beside the tint rather than under it. Widgets deep enough to resolve their own theme move anyway — which is exactly what makes it easy to miss: the phone layout looks perfect while the wide layout’s information pane sits alone on the untinted surface. Deleting the Builder fails that one test and nothing else in the file.

Three traps, checked in the code rather than assumed

Verified

The palette is quantized to 29 buckets, and four of them are neutral. A photo whose dominant bucket is #000000 or #cccccc seeds a dead scheme. Skip the neutrals; fall back to the ordinary theme when they are all neutral — a greyscale wallpaper getting no tint is correct, a greyscale wallpaper getting a muddy brown one is the bug.

The real 29, verbatim from wallpaper_color.dart. The struck four are unusable as seeds. Click any of the rest.

Verified

Konachan supplies no colours at all. Still open — L1a ships the fallback, not the fix. konachan_mapper.dart:110 returns an empty list, so L1 covers one source of two. Either accept it — the neutral fallback already handles it silently — or add L1b: extract a dominant colour from the 64px thumbnail decode the blurred backdrop already performs, behind a project-owned port. Ship L1a first and decide L1b on the device; the Konachan wall may not look wrong enough to pay for it.

Proposal

Seed the scheme, never paint the raw hex. _withAccent is the pattern that already exists: grow a tonal palette at the current brightness and splice only the brand roles. Painting a hex onto a surface directly is how you get unreadable text — the theme already refuses a brightness mismatch for exactly this reason.

Deferred

The immersive viewer is not in L1a. Its canvas is a hardcoded black and its chrome is black scrims, so nothing there reads the scheme at all. Tinting it means editing controls the private library and the download viewer share — its own slice, with its own device pass.

Watch on device

The backdrop is a blurred copy of the same image, so tint and backdrop will agree by construction — which risks the pane becoming one colour with no separation. The hairline edge already drawn for C2’s melt case may or may not be enough. That is a question for the tablet, not for an argument.

L2

Motion as a system

L2a Built Parallax Blocked — needs a call Tests 1348, from 1337 Splash Measured on device
Fixed

A tile’s ink splash was invisible — on every shelf, always. A Material paints its ink layer beneath its child, and every tile is an opaque BoxFit.cover image, so the splash was drawn under the wallpaper. Pressing a tile gave no feedback at all. The ink now sits on a transparent Material above the thumbnail. A fix, not a flourish, and older than this pass.

Measured

On the tablet: 108,107 pixels differ between a pressed and unpressed tile, and the tile’s mean brightness lifts 96.4 → 129.4. By eye, a soft even highlight across the pressed tile with its neighbour untouched. The ink genuinely was invisible before, and genuinely is not now.

Built

The wall arrives rather than appearing. A tile fades and lifts six percent of its own height over 260 ms, staggered 45 ms along a row. It fires once per wallpaper, not once per build — a shelf recycles its rows, so a tile scrolled off and back would otherwise re-animate and the wall would flicker under a scroll.

Today motion is a 220 ms fade, a Hero flight, a shimmer and a zoom animator — each correct, each reduce-motion aware, and none of them a system. Four additions, cheapest first:

Not observable

The entrance animation cannot be checked over adb, and that is a tooling limit, not a result. screencap runs about 300 ms per frame — coarser than the 260 ms arrival it would need to resolve. A cold-launch burst does show the wall brightening, but the frame before it is the empty wall still waiting on page one, so that step is the page arriving, not the fade. Four unit tests pin the behaviour; a human eye on a real scroll is still owed, especially at Small density where a row holds nine and the cascade caps at six.

Parallax is blocked, and it is your call

It cannot be built without contradicting the layout law. justified_rows.dart states it three times — “without cropping any of them”, “every tile keeps its true aspect ratio” — and the tile pours a BoxFit.cover image into a box shaped by exactly that ratio, so nothing is cropped today. Parallax needs the image taller than its box to have room to drift. That means cropping every thumbnail on the wall.

OptionWhat it costs
Drop itNothing. The entrance already gives the wall motion and the law stands.
Accept a ~6% cropPass B’s “nothing cropped” becomes false — and the uncropped thumb is why the tile fetches thumbOriginalUrl rather than Wallhaven’s fixed-crop small.
Parallax the backdrop (recommended)Detail’s blurred letterbox is cover by construction, so drifting it as the metadata scrolls costs no law at all. Much smaller effect, zero conflict.

The structural warning — this is the whole risk of the thread

Verified

The justified grid is a ListView.builder over rows, and each tile is a SizedBox inside a Row. Parallax needs every tile to know the scroll offset, and doing that with per-tile listeners is a rebuild storm across a wall holding hundreds of tiles. Drive it from one controller — or better, reposition without rebuilding at all.

Verified

The tile deliberately does not size itself. The row assigns its height, and tile density depends on that. Parallax must move the image inside the clip, never the tile.

Verified

Two live wires, both easy to cut by accident. Next-page fetch only runs off a scroll notification, and the underfill top-up exists precisely because a short shelf emits none — that was a real device defect, found at Small density on the tablet. If a parallax rewrite swallows or replaces those notifications, the wall silently stops paging. Re-run the underfilled-wall test and believe it.

L3

Detail as an editorial page, not a spec sheet

Built Phone only Tablet Keeps its two panes Device Passed, one defect fixed
Decided

The tablet did not get it, and that was the whole call. Full-bleed with a sheet over it is a phone pattern because a phone has nowhere to put anything beside a wallpaper. The tablet has 440dp of pane free, so a sheet would cover the thing it describes. The wide layout keeps its two panes and got a typographic pass instead — the fact grid stopped being eight boxed rows and became plates: tracked labels over values in the display face, on hairlines.

Fixed

The apply button was drawn under the system bar, label and icon cut clean off. Its own SafeArea reports nothing under a Scaffold with extendBodyBehindAppBar. Two wrong diagnoses first — it was not the absolute positioning, and not Samsung’s taskbar — both settled by deploying the previous build and looking. Fixed from viewPadding, verified on the device.

Fixed

The preview’s fullscreen button landed under the app bar’s actions. Eight existing tests caught it before a device could: a tap on fullscreen would have opened the share sheet.

C3 made the eight scalar facts speak one visual language, which fixed coherence and left the panel reading like a form. The stunning version is different in kind: full-bleed image behind everything, metadata riding a draggable sheet that rises over it, apply as a persistent action on the glass.

What it must not lose

  • C3’s decision that Uploader and Source are not facts. They are places you can go, and they group with the browser hand-off as actions. A redesign that quietly folds them back into a grid has undone the thread.
  • The per-fact test keys, which exist only so a test can prove a fact is a tile rather than a row.
  • Two layouts, and one has never been looked at. C3 shipped an expanded pane and a compact list; only the expanded one has been seen on hardware. A draggable sheet changes both, so the compact layout stops being “nothing suspected” and becomes the first thing to check.

The screen also carries the gallery session, the immersive hand-off, the copy hold and the apply button. Two of the largest test files point straight at it.

L4

A front page for Browse

Built & device-checked Sliver conversion Not needed Band sharpness Needs a call
Corrected

It did not cost a sliver conversion. I said it would; that was wrong. A header that scrolls with the wall is item 0 of the existing list — only a pinned or collapsing one forces slivers. All five shelves kept the list they had.

Rebuilt

The band is a WallpaperTile, and thirty failing tests forced that. The first cut was bespoke and broke every browse test that indexes the wall by tile. That was not test debt: a bespoke band re-implements the marks, the badge, the selection tick and the ink, then becomes an exception the user must learn. Composing the tile inherits all of it and changed no existing test. Thirty failures to zero by deleting code.

Your call

The band is visibly soft on a tablet — arithmetic, not a guess: it draws the same thumbnail the tiles use, so they downscale it while the band upscales it across 2560px. Accept it (a masthead is judged by opening it), fetch the full image for one wallpaper per listing (several MB for decoration), or stop running it full-bleed on a wide screen (which fights what it is for).

Browse opens on a search field, seven sorting tabs and a grid: a tool, not a destination. A featured full-bleed hero above the wall, plus the tag shelves the library already knows how to draw, gives the app a front door.

The honest cost, stated because it is why this thread is last in appeal

It puts a scroll between the user and the wall, on the home screen, in an app whose entire promise is browse → preview → set. This is the most debatable change in the pass and the one most likely to be reverted after a week of living with it. Consider it on the Latest tab only, so six of seven listings stay a pure wall.

Verified

The structural cost is a sliver conversion. The grid is a ListView.builder, not slivers, so a header that scrolls with the wall means converting the shared grid — which is also what would let a collapsing app bar work. That touches all five shelves and the skeleton. And do not give the skeleton a scroll view of its own: pass A3 already recorded why, and it throws.

What L must not re-litigate

Carried forward from the passes that settled them on hardware, so nobody spends a slice re-deriving:

  • Masonry is gone. Justified rows are the layout law.
  • No frosted glass on the app bar. Built, rejected on the tablet, replaced with an opaque gradient. A test asserts every gradient stop is opaque — dropping one below 1 is the exact bug that killed it.
  • C2’s scrim stays at .45, and C4’s pill alpha stays where it is. Both were argued from a real device, and both fixes-by-alpha would dull a case that is already right.
  • The brand mark is not the accent. The accent belongs to the user; identity must not move with it.

Before any of it

The clipboard work is merged and the tree is clean. Every thread here is observable — none can be called done on a green suite alone, so a device pass belongs in each slice, not at the end of the pass. And the tablet is reachable only from a terminal started outside the agent session; if the device list is empty, that is the ask.

Set in Bricolage Grotesque — the app’s own display face — over its splash ground #14122E, with the mark drawn from the normative geometry in assets/brand/tela_mark.svg.
